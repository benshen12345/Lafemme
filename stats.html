What do these numbers mean?
Evening gowns viewed
How many times someone looked closely at a gown — opening its photo, plus flipping through its colours. The small line on the card splits the two.
From the catalogue redesign of 17 Aug 2026 each colour has its own card, so there is no longer a row of colour dots to flip through. The looks did not stop happening — they moved from the "colour looks" half of this number into the "opened" half, because seeing a colour now means opening its own card. The total stays comparable across the change; the split does not. Over the 60 days to 17 Aug 2026 the two halves ran 1,385 opened against 1,210 colour looks on the gowns side, so expect the small line to shrink to just "opened" while the big number holds. Men suits are unaffected — every suit is a single colour, and its colour-look count was already zero.
Men suits viewed
The same thing on the suits side.
Visits
How many times the website was opened. The same person opening it twice counts twice, so read it as traffic, not as people.
Total reach-outs
Every time a customer started a conversation: chat button opens + price enquiries + booking forms sent. This is the number to watch — it's the closest thing to a lead.
Chat button opens
Taps on the general "chat with us" buttons — the floating WhatsApp button, the one in the Book-a-fitting section, and the showroom notice. Not tied to any piece.
Price enquiries
Taps on "Ask price" inside a piece's photo popup. Always tied to a specific piece, which is what makes it the strongest signal on the page.
Booking forms sent
How many times someone filled in the Book-a-fitting form and sent it through to WhatsApp. Only a completed send counts. Tapping "Book a fitting" inside a piece's popup does not count here — that only drops the piece into the fitting list and contacts nobody; it's shown on the small line under this number instead.
Recorded as its own event from 7 Aug 2026. Earlier days are reconstructed, not recorded: back then both actions shared one name, but the two wrote different fields — a form send carried no piece id, an "add to fitting list" did — so they can be told apart after the fact. Where a date range includes those days the small line says how many of the number came from that reconstruction. Over 20 Jul – 6 Aug it works out at 14 real sends inside 56 old "booking clicks", i.e. the retired tile was overstating leads about fourfold.
Individual visits
The "Visits" tab breaks the totals back down into single browsing sessions, so you can see whether anyone is genuinely engaging rather than just how many taps happened. A row is highlighted when that visit ended in a booking.
A page reload starts a new row — the site never stores anything on the visitor's device, so it cannot tell that two sessions are the same person. Rows a few minutes apart are almost always one person; read them together. "Screen: narrow" means under 760px wide, which is how the site records it — a laptop with a narrow window counts as narrow, a tablet held sideways does not.
Most viewed pieces
What customers opened most in this date range. The coloured dots under each row show which colours of that piece they actually looked at.
Most wanted colours
Colour popularity across the whole catalogue, not per piece.
What people searched
The exact words customers typed into the search box, most frequent first. A word that keeps coming up for something you don't carry is a demand signal.
Which filters they used
Which colours, styles, fabrics and brands customers tapped in the filter panel — what they were actively looking for.
Rental questions opened
Which rental FAQ questions customers expanded — deposit, fitting fee, cleaning, and so on. Heavy interest in one question tells you what's on people's minds before they book.
Highest intent — price enquiries
Views against price enquiries for each piece. A high ask rate means people who see it want it — worth restocking. Many views with no enquiries usually means the photo pulls people in but the piece doesn't close.

Not counted: scrolling past pieces, switching between the Gowns and Suits tabs, and using the filters. Only deliberate actions are recorded.

The numbers above are for the selected date range. Use the Pieces and What they wanted tabs for the detail behind them.

Individual visits

The tiles above are totals. This is the same data one visit at a time — which is where a real customer becomes visible. One evening in late July a single phone spent 47 minutes across three reloads, opened 84 pieces, shortlisted 26, asked the price of 3 and sent a booking. In the totals that is just "+1 booking".
A page reload starts a new row. Rows minutes apart on the same day are very likely one person — read them together, the way that 47-minute visit is really three rows. Only visits that opened at least one piece are listed, and "active for" is the span between the first and last thing they did in this table — not how long the tab sat open.
